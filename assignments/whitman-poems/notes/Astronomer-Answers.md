# When I Heard the Learn’d Astronomer

Tommy Wang

1. **What is the speaker literally doing? Where?**

The speaker first listens to an astronomer give a lecture in a lecture room. After becoming tired and sick, he leaves, wanders outside by himself, and looks up at the stars in silence.

2. **What causes the shift in the middle?**

The first four lines begin with "When" and describe the lecture. Line 5 breaks that pattern with "How soon" and reveals that the speaker feels tired and sick. He then moves from the crowded lecture room to the quiet outdoors, shifting from calculations to a personal experience of nature.

3. **What is the mood of the first half? The last half?**

The first half feels dull and overwhelming from the speaker’s point of view because the stars are reduced to facts and calculations. The last half feels peaceful and full of wonder as he experiences the night sky for himself.

4. **Explain interesting diction, sentence structure, or line length.**

Words like "proofs," "figures," and "charts" make the lecture sound technical and logical. The first four lines grow longer, piling up information like the lecture itself. Later, "gliding," "mystical," and "perfect silence" create a calmer feeling. The shorter final line gives the poem a quiet ending.

5. **Explain the irony and the placement of "stars."**

We expect a lecture about astronomy to make the speaker more interested in the stars, but it makes him feel sick and want to leave. He finds wonder only after going outside. Although the lecture is about stars, the word "stars" appears only as the poem’s last word. This delays our encounter with the actual stars until the speaker experiences them directly.

6. **How does the poem represent transcendental beliefs?**

The poem values self-reliance and a direct connection with nature. Even though the audience applauds the astronomer, the speaker trusts his own feelings and leaves. Looking at the stars himself gives him a sense of wonder that the lecture does not provide. This reflects the transcendental belief that personal experience and intuition can reveal truths beyond facts alone.
