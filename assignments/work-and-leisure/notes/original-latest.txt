As a new immigrant, I always ask myself: why do I come to America? In simple words, because of the American dream - everybody has an equal chance to win. Equal chance is the substantial factor of the reason why Americans are willing to challenge themselves no matter how arduous the task is. This leads to all the valuable ideas and works for human progression made by Americans. However, lots of Americans have a work balanced life by cultivating their own interests in downtime. "To be of use" by Marge Piercy is a poem that describes types of people who produce valuable works for the world. For example, Piercy appreciates people who persevere, collaborative and are self-motivated. "Leisure" by W. H. Davis is an ironic poem that satirizes the life which has no downtime for leisure is poor. Author Marge Piercy presents perseverance and committed work as valuable; however W. D. Davis complicates this idea by warning that a life centered only on productivity is empty and stressful when people lose time to appreciate nature.

In "To be of use", Marge Piercy communicates a key idea of American culture, which is that perseverance creates works that are meaningful to the world. He starts the poem by appreciating people who "jump into work headfirst". By admiring people who “jump into work headfirst”, Piercy presents meaningful work as something people are willing to embrace. Their readiness to begin a new work suggests that motivation comes from their own commitment rather than pressure from others. Therefore, it indicates that these people are self-motivated. Because perseverance is used to describe people who have willpower to do work by themselves and improve their works, this is exactly the group of people who are appreciated by the author. The author also appreciates another group of people "who do what has to be done, again and again", that indicates these people are able to master their work repeatedly. Because their persistence continues even after their initial enthusiasm has faded, perseverance is directly related to this group of people. Beginning a task with enthusiasm is easier than continuing even after excitement has faded. Finally author Piercy addresses how work is meaningful for the world by stating that, "Hopi vases that held corn, are put in the museum." These ancient clay jars used by Native Americans to store and carry liquids, are originally made to use. Because they are well made, they become art and put into the museum. Thus this indicates that works are meaningful because if people do their work with great perseverance and motivation, these works will be used and remembered forever like the hopi vases.

Although Piercy proposes that Americans do need the sense of tension, Davis reminds us that Americans also need to make time for enjoying nature due to the busy and stressful life they have. This is reinforced by author from using repetitions of "no time". The author repeats "no time" 7 times in the poem and states that people have no time to "stand beneath boughs" and see "streams full of stars". "Boughs" and "stars" are all natural scenarios. At the end of the poem, the author displays life is "a poor life if, full of care/ We have no time to stand and stare". By calling the existence of “a poor life,” Davis suggests that a person can fulfill daily responsibilities yet still lack a satisfying life. Here, “full of care” suggests being consumed by worries in everyday life, leaving little attention for the beauty nearby, which is further explained when people have "no time to stand and stare". As a result this type of life being described is refused by the author because he describes it as "poor". In other words, author Davis believes people need to make time for seeing natural scenarios and experiencing nature in order to live a full life.

Together, the poems suggest that Americans should value both meaningful work and the ability to experience life. This can be seen when self-motivated and persevering people contribute useful work to the world. Also, another equally important point, people also need to experience nature, can be seen when author W. D. Davis categorizes life with no time to see beauties of nature as a poor life. In world today, significance of downtime is emphasized by researchers Patricia Albulescu and Irina Macsinga in their "Give me a break! A systematic review and meta-analysis on the efficacy of Micro-breaks for increasing well-being and performance" on August 21, 2022. They study effects of short breaks on people's performance of work. They found out that downtime help people to perform better in work afterward by reducing fatigue, and longer break improves more. Instead of seeing downtime as waste of time, it actually supports people's long term perseverance and meaningful work. Overall, challenging ourselves and consistent working are still important; however, people are not machines, so people also need some downtime.
