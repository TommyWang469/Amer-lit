# Meaningful Work and a Full Life

As a new immigrant, I often ask myself why I came to America. One answer is the American dream: the belief that opportunity and hard work can lead to a better life. This belief encourages people to challenge themselves and create things that benefit others. However, building a better life also means having time to enjoy it. Marge Piercy's poem "To be of use" values people who approach necessary work with energy and commitment. In "Leisure," W. H. Davies questions whether a busy life is truly fulfilling when people cannot pause to appreciate the world around them. Although Piercy presents perseverance and useful work as sources of satisfaction, Davies complicates this idea by suggesting that achievement alone cannot make life meaningful if people lose the ability to enjoy everyday beauty.

In "To be of use," Piercy presents perseverance as valuable because it allows people to contribute something useful to the world. Her admiration for those who "jump into work head first" suggests that meaningful work begins with a willingness to become fully involved. Rather than waiting for someone else to take responsibility, these people commit their own energy to the task. Yet starting with enthusiasm is only part of that commitment. By praising people who complete necessary work "again and again," Piercy shifts attention from the excitement of beginning to the patience required to continue. Repetitive tasks may bring little immediate recognition, but doing them reliably meets needs that do not disappear when the work becomes boring or difficult. This connection between effort and usefulness becomes clearer through the "Hopi vases that held corn." Although these objects are now displayed in museums, Piercy emphasizes that they originally served a practical purpose. Their value comes from more than being admired: they were made to meet people's everyday needs. The vases suggest that ordinary work can be worthy of respect because of what it provides for others. Through these examples, Piercy offers a way to understand success beyond personal recognition, showing that satisfaction can come from making a dependable contribution.

Although Piercy proposes that Americans do need the sense of tension, Davis reminds us that Americans also need to make time for enjoying nature due to the busy and stressful life they have. The repeated phrase "no time" makes each missed experience feel like part of a larger pattern. People pass through a world filled with beauty, yet their attention remains occupied by other demands. When Davies describes "streams full of stars," the image suggests that something as ordinary as sunlight on water is worth a moment of attention. Enjoyment does not always require an expensive vacation or a major achievement; it can come from noticing what is already nearby. At the end of the poem, the author displays life is "a poor life if, full of care/ We have no time to stand and stare". By calling the existence of “a poor life,” Davis suggests that a person can fulfill daily responsibilities yet still lack a satisfying life. Here, “full of care” suggests being consumed by worries in everyday life, leaving little attention for the beauty nearby, which is further explained when people have "no time to stand and stare". As a result this type of life being described is refused by the author because he describes it as "poor". In other words, author Davis believes people need to make time for seeing natural scenarios and experiencing nature in order to live a full life.

Together, the poems suggest that the better life promised by the American dream should include both useful contributions and time to enjoy the world. Piercy helps explain why commitment matters, while Davies raises the question of what that commitment should ultimately make possible. Modern research also supports taking people's need for rest seriously. In their 2022 review, "Give me a break!," Patricia Albulescu, Irina Macsinga, and their colleagues found that short breaks reduced fatigue and increased energy, although the overall improvement in work performance was not statistically significant. These findings suggest that rest deserves attention because it supports people's well-being, even when it does not clearly increase productivity. For me, this balance changes what it means to pursue the American dream. I want my effort to lead to useful work, but I also want enough time and attention to enjoy the life I am building.
