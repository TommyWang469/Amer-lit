# The user's writing style

Use with [the essay guide](ESSAY_WRITING_GUIDE.md) and [the commentary guide](LITERARY_COMMENTARY_GUIDE.md). The goal is a clearer, stronger version of the user's own writing. This profile is based mainly on [their initial work-and-leisure essay](../assignments/work-and-leisure/notes/original-initial.txt), not a complete profile of everything they write.

## Voice to preserve

- Use direct explanations and familiar, high-school-level vocabulary. Keep precise literary terms when useful. Avoid language that sounds like a textbook, a speech, or an adult imitating a teenager.
- Preserve visible cause-and-effect reasoning: explain why an observation leads to an interpretation. Improve the connection rather than simply lengthening the sentence.
- Connect literary ideas to everyday values, choices, and experiences when the argument calls for it.
- Preserve the user's perspective and wording that is already clear. A personal question can open an essay when it fits the prompt and comes from facts the user supplied.
- Keep the user's balanced reasoning: one idea may be valuable while another text reveals its limits or another need. Do not turn the comparison into two unrelated summaries.
- Use a natural mix of sentence lengths. Let transitions express actual relationships instead of forcing "therefore," "finally," or "in other words" into every explanation.

The user's chat spelling and abbreviations are not the model for school essays. Do not force slang, dramatic phrases, or advanced synonyms. Avoid filler such as "this serves as a powerful reminder." Stronger analysis matters more than impressive vocabulary.

## Humanize from the user's actual writing

Compare a draft with the original sample: would this sound like the user explaining the idea more clearly? Preserve their familiar language, personal perspective, and way of building an explanation. Replace generic, overly formal phrasing with clear wording they can understand and explain themselves.

Do not invent personal experiences, emotions, cultural details, or facts to make writing feel personal. Do not promise AI-detection outcomes or present assistant-written material as independently authored by the user. Later drafts that contain assistant suggestions are not fresh evidence of the user's natural voice.

## Grammar: lightly edited, not completely polished

The user explicitly prefers a few minor, natural grammatical imperfections. When revising their writing, small article, preposition, or phrasing issues may remain if the meaning is clear. Do not turn every sentence into polished academic English or impose a random error count.

Fix confusing sentences, errors that change the intended meaning, and grammar that obscures the thesis or reasoning. Keep quotations, author names, source information, and facts accurate. Base light editing on the actual samples, not a stereotype of an English learner. Do not scatter arbitrary errors through an essay. Follow an explicit proofreading or grammar-correction request when given.

This is a voice preference, not a grading advantage: a teacher may still deduct for remaining errors.

## Creative writing and source-based work

- Read the assignment, source pages, and supplied handwritten notes. Preserve the user's characters, intended events, and message; distinguish new creative suggestions from supplied details.
- Use specific actions, believable reactions, and natural dialogue. Avoid having characters explain the theme in speeches or repeating the moral after the ending.
- Check cause and effect and the order of events. Do not present an invented story as an authentic account from a real culture.
- Record assignment-process rules honestly. If AI-written work is prohibited, label a generated draft as reference material and identify the unmet requirement.

## Voice check before delivery

Read for natural rhythm and clarity. Keep the user's perspective and familiar wording while removing repetitive explanations. Confirm the result sounds like a stronger version of their supplied writing and that every personal detail actually comes from them.
