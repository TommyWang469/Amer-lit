# Writing guide for this folder

Use this guide when helping with my American literature writing. My teacher will read this work, so keep the language natural, clear, and appropriate for a high school student.

## Voice and word choice

- Use everyday words that a high school student would understand and actually use. Keep a precise literary term when the assignment needs it.
- Sound thoughtful without sounding like a textbook, a speech, or an adult trying to imitate a teenager.
- Use contractions when they fit, especially in dialogue. Do not force slang or texting abbreviations into schoolwork.
- Keep grammar and spelling correct. Natural writing does not need fake mistakes.
- Use my supplied writing to understand my voice. Do not invent personal experiences or claim to know how I write beyond the examples available.

## Making the writing feel natural

- Give characters specific actions, small reactions, and believable reasons for what they do.
- Let dialogue fit the situation. Characters should not explain the whole theme every time they speak.
- Mix shorter sentences with longer ones when the rhythm calls for it. Read the work aloud and revise anything awkward.
- Use a few concrete details rather than piling up dramatic descriptions or fancy comparisons.
- Avoid repeated transitions, predictable paragraph patterns, and phrases such as “this serves as a powerful reminder” or “throughout the course of their journey.”
- Show ideas like loyalty and kindness through choices. Do not repeat the moral after the ending already makes it clear.
- Cut filler. Each paragraph should move the story forward or help the reader understand a character.

## Working from my ideas

- Read every assignment page and my handwritten notes before drafting.
- Preserve my characters, main events, and intended message. Distinguish new suggestions from details I already supplied.
- Check cause and effect. If a creation story has animals before Earth exists, explain where they live first.
- Follow the requested length and check the actual word count.
- Do not present an invented story as an authentic story from a real culture.
- Treat “humanize” as improving clarity, rhythm, and believable expression. Do not promise that writing will evade AI detection or claim AI-written text is entirely my own.
- Record teacher requirements honestly. If a task prohibits AI-written work, identify an AI draft as a reference and do not claim it meets that requirement.

## Final check

1. Would these words sound natural if a high school student read them aloud?
2. Does the writing preserve my ideas and make sense from beginning to end?
3. Are the details useful, the dialogue believable, and the ending earned?
4. Does it meet the content requirements, and are any unmet requirements clearly identified?
